# Portfolio — Experience

## Scope

The single-page portfolio: home, about, education, experience, projects, skills, contact, and navigation.

## Audience and job

Prospective employers and freelance clients assess Emil’s frontend and UI/UX work, then contact him.

## Content and constraints

Use the supplied biography, portrait, education and experience records, projects, skills, URLs, and contact flow. Do not invent project screenshots, outcomes, testimonials, or credentials. The profile portrait is the only supplied photograph.

## Direction contract

**THESIS:** Make the portfolio feel like an online exhibition of Emil’s real work, where the projects are the exhibits and the visitor can move through them at their own pace. Refuse the standard developer dashboard and thumbnail-card wall.

**OWN-WORLD:** Deep ink gallery walls, warm white lettering, restrained vermilion markers, and crisp wall-label typography. Real work and portrait photography carry the visual weight; no fabricated project imagery or decorative gradients.

**STORY:** Visitors meet Emil and his current practice, inspect selected projects and their real links, follow education and experience, scan tools, then reach a working contact form.

**FIRST VIEWPORT:** An open two-column exhibition: Emil’s name, role, concise supplied introduction, and two clear actions on the left; his portrait on the right, treated like one framed work with a small factual location label. The first selected project title and its real demo link appear at the lower edge of the viewport.
